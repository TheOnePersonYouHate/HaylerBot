Used properly, this ship can wield off attacks from any number of threats and hit back with tremendous alacrity. Put simply, Hayler is a big ship, with big arms to back it up. Sensors In the age of sail, the spyglass was king, capable of identifying ships out to the horizon. In the dreadnought age, the rangefinder replaced it, but was still operated by the almighty Mark 1 Eyeball. Only in the Second World War did radar replace the eyes as a warship’s main tool of detection. Nowadays, modern warships are utterly reliant on these electromagnetic devices to do much of anything. Hayler is no different; she has the sharpest eyes in the world, regardless of what the AWACS pilots say. Here we will discuss the various sensors of an Arleigh Burke-class Flight III destroyer, and how they make these ships the deadliest in the world. Radar AN/SPY-6V1 This is the heart of Hayler’s air defense capabilities. The four AN/SPY-6 phased-array radars mounted at the corners of the ship’s superstructure are designed to search the sky and sea for threats out to nearly four hundred nautical miles. Their positioning on the ship’s superstructure does mean that their radar horizon is somewhat low (this will be important later), but this system is excellent at tracking high-altitude targets and is a major part of Ballistic Missile Defense. DO NOT STAND IN FRONT OF THE RADAR FACES WHEN EMITTING! They can severely harm you with microwave radiation! If you think you’re tougher than a kitchen appliance, please remember that the Navy once considered using SPY-1D as a directed energy weapon due to its power output. Do not fuck with the danger plates. A brief word about phased-array radars: these radars use hundreds of smaller emitters fixed to the gray face you see. This has the benefit of giving phenomenal resolution and simultaneous tracking and engagement capabilities, but at the cost of needing multiple faces to cover 360 degrees around the ship. This is a vastly simplified explanation and is likely wrong by virtue of that, but as you’re not designing new radar sets, it’s not necessary to go into excruciating detail.

AN/SPQ-9B The direction radar for Hayler’s five-inch gun. The system gives range and bearing for surface and low-altitude targets, allowing the gun to track and fire on targets quickly. SPQ-9B has twice the range of the older SPQ-9A and significantly improved resolution due to its vastly more modern antenna. AN/SPG-62 As important to Hayler’s defenses as SPY-6, AN/SPG-62 designates targets for active and semi-active radar-homing missiles, allowing the former to track to seeker range and providing terminal guidance to the latter. Each antenna can direct multiple missiles simultaneously, and Hayler has three mounted on her fore and aft superstructure. These illuminators can “timeshare”, allowing missiles to be directed against multiple contacts as they arrive in the area of the target.

Anti-Submarine Systems AN/SQS-53C Sonar Array Hayler’s hull-mounted active and passive sonar, used to listen in on submarines at great distances. Passive sonar (alt. hydrophones) are used to listen for enemy submarine contacts, while active sonar (pings) are used to locate submarines for attacks. Passive sonar WILL see a nearby target, but will clearly mark the user’s location as well, while hydrophones can be spoofed or will not pick up a target that is too quiet. Active sonar can acquire targets within fifty miles. DO NOT ACTIVATE SONAR IF THERE IS A RISK OF NEARBY SWIMMERS! It’s not just a funny noise or a small fishfinder; the sound waves emitted by the SQS-53 can kill or injure human beings through organ damage and have been known to kill full-size whales within a few miles. Do not swim near the sonar bulge at any time.

Weapons Gun Mountings 5”/62 Mark 45 Mod 4 Lightweight Gun The big gun mounted on Hayler’s bow is her 5-inch gun (Mark 45 Mod 4). It’s an evolutionary improvement on the baseline Mark 45, possessing a longer barrel (L/62 instead of L/54) and a stealthy gunhouse. The gun is more accurate and longer-ranged than earlier Mods. Right now, it possesses HE-FRAG and HE rounds with programmable fuses capable of engaging airborne and surface targets out to fifteen miles. In the future, hypervelocity and guided rounds will be available as standard munitions for land and sea combat. Please stand clear while firing or your hearing loss will be service related. Phalanx CIWS Block 1B The last line of defense against inbound anti-ship missiles, Hayler possesses two of the US Navy’s standard Close-In Weapon System. The Phalanx is built around the 20mm M61 Vulcan and a custom fire control system located in a dome above the gun (which contains the independent search/track/gunlaying radars as seen above). The system can be set to fully automatic, which enables it to automatically track, engage, and fire five thousand rounds per minute at an incoming target. Hayler received a second CIWS in crash Enhanced Lethality Program due to rising global tensions; the standard outfit for a Burke is just one Phalanx.

Long-Range Anti-Ship Missile (RGM-158C) Type: Anti-Ship Cruise Missile Speed: Mach 0.9 Range: >500 miles Guidance: GPS, Dual Active/Passive radar homing and IIR Once canceled as a surface weapon, LRASM was introduced during the Russo-American War to give American vessels a way to strike back against Russian ships with Kalibr and Onyx anti-ship missiles. Though nowhere near as fast as either, the missile’s low RCS made it nearly impossible to intercept by older Russian ships and its heavy warhead ensured that even one hit could kill a warship. LRASM was mostly co-opted by the USAF and carrier air wings postwar, and its high price encouraged acquisition of Joint Strike Missile instead. However, enough examples remain in Navy inventory to arm a significant number of ships.

Joint Strike Missile (RGM-184A) Type: Anti-Ship Cruise Missile Speed: Mach 0.9 Range: >150 miles Guidance: GPS and radar-aided LWIR + Dual Active/Passive radar homing The US Navy’s standard vertical launch anti-ship missile. JSM was initially designed to be fired internally from the F-35, but changes to the Naval Strike Missile airframe allowed it to fit into Mark 41 cells. Post-Russian War, the lower cost and smaller RCS of this missile when compared to LRASM ensured its place in the USN. It has a larger warhead and longer range than the NSM it was derived from, though it admittedly lacks the reach of LRASM. Both LRASM and JSM are capable of networking to engage enemy warships and striking them in specific locations to maximize damage.

Aegis Combat System The engines are Hayler’s heart, and her weapons are her arms, but AEGIS is Hayler’s brain. Put simply, AEGIS takes sensor inputs and outputs them as firepower-based solutions, identifying threats and deciding how to deal with them faster than human operators could. This enables the ship to track, engage, and intercept inbound weapons automatically with whatever is deemed necessary, integrating the entire ship as one weapon. This decreases response time to threats and ensures that the right weapon is always online for use. AEGIS is capable of fully automated engagements, but this is not recommended for fear of friendly fire.

Corvettes Tarantul III-class Type 056A One of the most common types of ships in the world, corvettes are the lightest “fleet” combatants and come in a variety of flavors. Many serve as low-end escorts for fleet units or convoys, while others are anti-submarine patrol ships and still others serve as oversized missile boats for coastal defense. This is an extremely diverse ship classification and a full analysis of the type would take far too long, so independent research is advised. Selected types: Oceangoing light escort (ex: Type 056A, Steregushchiy-class, Kamorta-class) - A general-purpose type, usually capable of carrying helicopters and providing local anti-aircraft and anti-submarine defense. 4-8 anti-ship missiles are usually carried, along with a 40-100mm gun. Think of them as small, less-capable frigates. Many are also used for longer patrols of littoral regions if greater firepower than a simple submarine chaser is required. Littoral submarine chaser (ex: Grisha-class, Sariwon-class, Type 037 variants) - A dedicated littoral submarine-hunter, generally very poorly armed for anything else. Most ships of this type have 76-57mm guns and no long-range anti-ship or anti-aircraft weaponry. This type is increasingly falling out of favor as submarine weapons increase in lethality; most surviving examples are very old ex-Soviet or Chinese designs and are used in a similar role to US Coast Guard cutters. Coast defense ship (ex: Tarantul-class, Kora-class, Shahid Soleimani-class) - A Soviet-pioneered idea, scaling up the concept of a missile boat with substantially heavier armament normally mounted on destroyers. These ships pose a significant threat in littoral waters and carry anywhere from four to sixteen anti-ship missiles.

They are completely incapable of high-seas combat due to their small size, but generally have similar anti-ship firepower to corvettes and frigates. Their high firepower for their low cost makes them ideal for maritime defense of poorer nations or large nations with long coastlines. Type 022 - The most modern Chinese missile boat, armed with an AK-630 and eight YJ-83 anti-ship missiles. Built to a unique catamaran configuration, this class is particularly stealthy and fast, making them deadly in inshore work. Sina-class - An Iranian FAC with a single 76mm gun and four C-802 anti-ship missiles. This version of a fast attack craft (medium gun, light missiles) is common in littoral navies.

Aircraft Types and Recognition Fighter Aircraft Shenyang J-11 (PRC) F-4 Phantom (Iran) Fighters are designed for combat against other aircraft. Generally, true fighters lack the payload and targeting systems for ground and sea-attack missions, and as such are little threat to warships. However, this should NEVER be taken for granted, as even one lucky hit can sink a ship and most fighters nowadays have multirole functionality. Dedicated fighters will generally not be targeted by Hayler in a standard mission unless the ship is supporting ground troops and degrading enemy air superiority. Please note that fighters, with their excellent maneuverability, are better at dodging SAMs than most other aircraft, especially with advance warning. A subset of fighters are interceptors. Interceptors generally have excellent speed and poor maneuverability, being designed to take down bombers and not engage other fighters on their own terms. Very few nations have dedicated interceptors. Relevant examples: - F-14: ex-American interceptors, used exclusively by Iran. - F-4: similar to above, but far less capable. Some fitted for ground strike. - F-5E/F: very light fighters with limited A2G capability. - J-11: a standard PLAAF air superiority fighter, based on the Su-27. - MiG-21/J-7: very old fighters used extensively by less capable air forces. - MiG-29: a lightweight multirole fighter used by many Russian-aligned nations.

Electronic Warfare Aircraft The bane of any air warfare ship, EW aircraft carry powerful jammers to interfere with enemy radar systems and prevent targeting of an inbound raid package. While it is possible to burn through the electronic noise they emit, this takes time and is only easy at shorter ranges. Few dedicated EW aircraft exist in the modern day, as most multirole aircraft can carry EW jammer pods alongside weapons; the Su-24MP of the RUAF and J-15D of the PLAN are the most consistent examples. There are too many examples to count, each optimized for a different role. If a plane can be used to observe ships at sea, it can be a maritime patrol aircraft. Weapon Types and Roles Types Cruise Missile A cruise missile is a missile capable of sustaining powered level flight, receiving external or onboard guidance. Cruise missiles are generally powered by turbojet or turbofan engines, though supersonic ramjet-powered versions are increasingly common. Level flight missiles powered by rocket engines are generally not considered true cruise missiles, as they (usually) lack the range of their jet-powered counterparts but are significantly simpler and cheaper. Cruise missiles are more difficult to track than ballistic missiles and offer substantially less warning due to their lower flight path, but are generally much easier to intercept due to their lower speed. Ballistic Missile Ballistic missiles fly in an extremely high trajectory (often hundreds of miles into the atmosphere), delivering warheads at speeds in excess of Mach 5. This makes them extremely difficult to intercept with conventional weapons - Hayler is one of relatively few ships equipped to do so. Typically, Ballistic Missiles lack any active guidance save for inertial or GPS assistance and are not accurate enough to engage maneuvering warships. Most have detachable reentry vehicles capable of just enough maneuvering to hit independent, stationary targets. Hypersonic Glide Vehicles Introduced in the early 2020s, HGVs “skip” across the upper atmosphere at very high speed but at a much lower altitude than ballistic missiles. Currently, only the Russian Federation is known to operate them and only in a land-attack role, though many other nations (including the US, China, and North Korea) are developing them. Unpowered Guided Munitions This section covers guided, unpowered munitions, like bombs, artillery shells, and rockets. These weapons come in three flavors (generally): laser, electro-optical and GPS-guided munitions, with some IR-guided weapons existing. The former two weapons need external targeting assistance from a nearby platform such as an aircraft or drone; laser guidance typically tracks a singular laser spot, while E/O guidance detects a target on a camera and follows said target until impact. Both are near-impossible to jam, but have the downside of needing a nearby launch or guidance platform, making them impractical against modern warships. GPS guidance is generally incapable of directly targeting warships, but it can be aided by a backup IR seeker.
